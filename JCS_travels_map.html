On the evidence. Blue marks a place a surviving record puts him — a muster roll, a hospital register, a clothing receipt, a prisoner exchange roll, an oath of allegiance. Orange marks a place his regiment demonstrably was while the two-month muster roll covering that window recorded him "present" — an inference, and a weaker one than it looks: the roll for November and December 1862 also calls him "present," while four other records in his own file have him in a Richmond hospital for seven of those nine weeks. Grey dashes mark a stretch nobody can trace: John's journey back from Savannah in November 1864, which the family compiler reconstructed from the railroads still running, and James's road east to the Cape Fear, which his muster rolls never name. The grey diamond at Camp Winder is not a contradiction but a boundary: John's mark is on a clothing receipt there on 9 June 1863, and no register of patients survives for Winder's Third Division to say when he left. Shape carries the brother — circles are John, squares are James — so colour is left free to carry the evidence. The two routes touch only once, at Point Lookout, where both men swore the oath on 19 June 1865 and were released under the same order.

Sources. Compiled service records and the family compilation Service Record of John C. Simmons; the 1927 North Carolina death certificate; and the dictated 1913 reminiscences of E. A. Patterson, a private in the same company, held by the State Archives of North Carolina. Both brothers' compiled service records were read in full from the original card images in August 2026 — John's filed as "Simmons, John," James's split between "Simmons, James C" and, for 1865, a variant jacket filed as "Simmon, C Jas." Distance is the sum of great-circle hops between consecutive stops — a floor, not the distance walked. Where stops fall within a few miles of each other — Rappahannock Station and Bealeton, and the Richmond–Petersburg–Fort Stedman–City Point cluster — the markers are nudged slightly apart so each stays legible and clickable; the routes between them are drawn as arcs, not as the roads taken.
