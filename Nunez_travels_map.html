The Nunez Branch

Five generations, from a silver town in Baja California to the Carolina coast — 1749 to 2006.

Every place on this map is fixed by a document. The colour of a marker says how firmly.

Documented — a record places them here Indexed or inferred — a transcript, or a household record Contested — the claim cannot stand as written The crossing — no record at all

The stops

In order of the family's movement, not of discovery. Sources are given for each.

El FuerteSinaloa, Mexico c. 1749 Andrés de Cota y León Recorded birthplace — and the point where the documented line stops. Every tree says he was born here about 1749. El Fuerte's only parish, Sagrado Corazón, did not begin keeping registers until 1783 — thirty-four years too late. The record that would name his parents does not survive, and the generations above him rest on nothing but other people's compilations.FamilySearch catalog 264059 (Sagrado Corazón, El Fuerte — registers 1783–1968)
LoretoBaja California Sur 24 Mar 1769 Andrés de Cota y León Marched out with the Portolá expedition — probably. The published roster of the 1769 expedition lists 27 soldados de cuera under Captain Rivera y Moncada of the Presidio of Loreto, and among the 24 known names is Andrés de Cota — in the same company as Sergeant José Francisco Ortega. He would have been twenty. The expedition founded San Diego and first sighted San Francisco Bay from land. The roster proves a man of that name was there; it does not prove it was this one, and a Pablo Antonio Cota is on the same list.Roster via Californio Ancestry, from Bolton, Fray Juan Crespí and Tibesar, Writings of Junípero Serra
San AntonioBaja California Sur 1819 – 1884 Three generations of Cotas The family seat. Three marriages in the parish register of San Antonio de Padua. A silver-mining town in the cape region. José María Cota married María Claudia Nuñes here on 9 February 1819; their son Andrés married María del Rosario Cota here on 31 May 1855; and José María — widowed in 1850 — remarried here in February 1851. The registers style them Dn. José María and Da Claudia Nuñes: Don and Doña. In a Baja mining town that marks property, office or a stake in the silver.Baja California Catholic Church Records, film 004400841 — entry 94 (1819) and entry 496 (1855)
La PazBaja California Sur 1842 – 1843 José Andrés Nuñes · Guadalupe Romero · Tirso Where the surname enters the family, and where the man who carried it to Florida was born. On 5 April 1842 Andrés married Guadalupe Romero at Nuestra Señora de la Paz — and he married under the name Joze Andres Nunes, his mother's surname, not his father's Cota. Ten months later, on 28 January 1843, their son Tirso was born; he was baptised here on 7 February. That is why the boy was Tirso Nuñes, and why the name reached Pensacola, Wilmington, and this family.Marriage: rec. 900079836, p.108. Baptism: rec. 79285, p.43, entry 326.
WarringtonEscambia Co., Florida 1870 – 1880 The Kimball family A New England carpenter's household at the gates of the Pensacola Navy Yard. Hazen D. Kimball, born in Concord, New Hampshire, appears in the 1870 census at Pensacola as a carpenter. By 1880 the family is at Warrington — the Navy Yard town — and his wife Celesta is head of household, describing herself as a widow. Their son Osmond was born in New York about 1864, between two Florida-born siblings: the family went north in the middle of the war and came back.1870 census, M593_129 p.622B · 1880 census, Warrington, ED 41 p.8d
PensacolaEscambia Co., Florida 1880 – 1911 George Tirso Nuñez & Edith Kimball A licensed bar pilot, a house owned free of mortgage, and a man steadily rewriting where he came from. Celesta Kimball died on 15 September 1880. Twenty-three days later, on 8 October, her eighteen-year-old daughter Edith married George Nunes. He worked as a pilot — one of the licensed men who took deep-draught ships across the Pensacola bar, a closed and well-paid trade. By 1900 he owned 5 Magazine Street outright and employed a live-in servant. He also told the 1885 census he was born in Spain, and the 1900 census that he was born in California to a Spanish father and an Italian mother. He was born in La Paz. He died here on 17 October 1909.Marriage 8 Oct 1880, FL County Marriage Records · 1885 FL state census, NARA M845 roll 3 p.1161 · 1900 census, roll 168 ED 12 p.16 · 1908 city directory, 431 E Intendencia St
Baker CountyMacclenny, Florida 30 Apr 1927 John Brew Nunez & Vera Evelyn Carty An elopement, thirty miles the wrong side of Jacksonville. Vera was seventeen. They crossed two county lines to get a licence, which is not a neutral administrative fact — the county line is the point. Their first daughter was born ten months later.Florida, U.S., County Marriage Records, 1823–1982
JacksonvilleDuval Co., Florida 1910s – 1965 John Brew Nunez & family A homeowner at twenty-three, dead at thirty-two. John was born about 1907 to an unmarried Elizabeth Nuñez and raised, aged three, in his grandmother's household back in Pensacola. By 1920 he was in Jacksonville living as John Flanders. By 1930 he was John Nuney again — twenty-three years old, married, a labourer for the city, and owner of 567 Chestnut Street, valued at $2,100 in the first year of the Depression. He died here in 1939. His mother Elizabeth, who had lived as Elizabeth Flanders for forty-five years, died here in May 1965.1920 census, T625_217 ED 49 p.13B · 1930 census, ED 63 p.7A · Florida Death Index, 1877–1998
KnoxvilleKnox Co., Tennessee 4 Sep 1909 Vera Evelyn Carty Birthplace of the woman who moved the family to North Carolina. Daughter of Edward Godfrey Carty and Leah Rebecca Free, in the 1920 census at Knoxville Ward 1. She married into the Nunez family at seventeen, was widowed at twenty-nine, and remarried.Tennessee, U.S., Delayed Birth Records, 1869–1909 · 1920 census, Knoxville Ward 1
HickoryCatawba Co., North Carolina by 1950 Vera Carty Bryan & Beverly Anne Nunez Why this family is in North Carolina at all. Widowed in Jacksonville in 1939 with two young daughters, Vera remarried John Calvin Bryan and by the 1950 census was living at 545 22nd Street in Hickory. Her younger daughter Beverly, twenty, was working as a clerical worker in the auto-parts trade, forty-four hours a week. Vera died here in 1982 and is buried in Hickory. Her elder daughter was not in that 1950 household — she had already left.1950 census, roll 4568 ED 18-17 p.17 · North Carolina Death Indexes, 1908–2004
WilmingtonNew Hanover Co., North Carolina 1969 – 2006 Vera Eleanor Nunez The end of the line, five generations and 2,400 miles from La Paz. Born in Jacksonville in March 1928, Vera Eleanor married Quez E. Simmons, who died here in 1969, and later William Basil Conway, who died in 1996. She died in Wilmington on 20 December 2006 and is buried here. She was eleven when her own father died, and she is the point at which the Nuñez name of a Baja California pilot entered the Simmons family.Social Security Death Index · Find a Grave · Obituary Daily Times Index
Fort WorthTarrant Co., Texas 12 Aug 1945 Minnie Virginia Nunez Henson Where a daughter of the Pensacola household ended up — and the source of a long-standing error. Minnie died here aged fifty-two. Her Texas death certificate names her father as George Nuney and her mother as Edith Kimble — and because Edith is named on it, somebody attached this death to Edith herself. The mother's recorded death has been wrong ever since. Where Edith actually died is unknown; she was last seen in the 1911 Pensacola directory, a widow of forty-eight.Texas, U.S., Death Certificates, 1903–1982
BarnwellSouth Carolina 1 Aug 1973 Venice Todd Nunez Hutto Another of the eight Pensacola children, scattered east. Born in Florida on 10 March 1895, she lived to seventy-eight and died in South Carolina.South Carolina, U.S., Death Records, 1821–1974
CocoaBrevard Co., Florida 1 Jan 1975 George Nunez A son who kept the name — and the male line that can still be tested. Born in Florida in July 1890, he died on the Atlantic coast in 1975. The given name George Tierso Nunez was still being handed to sons in Polk County into the 1980s. Because the surname reached this family through two women, only a male-line descendant of these sons can Y-test the Cota descent.Find a Grave · Florida marriage and divorce indexes, Polk Co., 1952–1989
ConcordNew Hampshire · not on map 14 Jul 1823 Hazen Deloran Kimball The New England end of the family — and the reason there were Kimballs in Florida. A carpenter born in Concord who turns up in Pensacola. New England shipyard tradesmen followed the work, and the Pensacola Navy Yard employed civilian carpenters. His daughter Edith married the Baja-born pilot.1870 census birthplace, New Hampshire · New Hampshire Marriage Records Index
BathMaine · not on map 25 Sep 1901? Hazen Deloran Kimball A recorded death that cannot be right — or is the wrong man. The tree has him dying in this shipbuilding town in 1901. But his wife Celesta was recorded as a widow, and head of her own household, in June 1880 — twenty-one years earlier. Either the Maine death belongs to a different Hazen Kimball, or Celesta was a deserted wife calling herself a widow, which required no paperwork and was the respectable thing to do. Unresolved.Contradicted by 1880 census, Warrington, ED 41 p.8d (Celesta Kimble, widowed, head of household)

Reading the map. The blue line is the direct line of descent. It breaks between La Paz and Pensacola because nothing survives from those thirty-seven years — the man who crossed was a mariner, which is the likeliest explanation for how, but not evidence of it. Orange dotted lines mark people marrying into the family. A few markers are nudged off their true position so they do not sit on top of each other; a hairline shows where each one actually belongs.

The one diamond on the map is a broken claim, not an uncertain place. El Fuerte is where every tree says Andrés de Cota y León was born about 1749 — but that parish's registers do not begin until 1783, so no record of the birth can survive, and everything above him rests on other people's compilations. Two stops sit in the table but not on the map: Concord, New Hampshire and Bath, Maine. Both are New England and both belong to the Kimballs, who married in; plotting them stretched the frame across half a continent for two points and buried the migration this map is actually about.
