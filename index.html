Simmons Family Research

Migration maps built from primary records — two branches, two continents

Each map plots where an ancestor can actually be placed, and marks how firmly. Colour and shape carry the strength of the evidence rather than decorating it, so the gaps in the record stay visible instead of being smoothed over.

The travels of John C. Simmons

4 July 1862 – 19 June 1865 · 30 stops · 2,366 miles

A private of Company E, 57th North Carolina Infantry, from enlistment at Newton to release from Point Lookout. Fifteen of his thirty-five months in service were spent as a prisoner of war; he was captured twice. Interactive — every stop opens its source.

The Nunez branch

1749 – 2006 · 16 stops · Baja California to Wilmington

Six generations from the cape region of Baja California to the North Carolina coast, by way of Pensacola and Jacksonville. Includes the thirty-seven-year gap between La Paz in 1843 and Pensacola in 1880, for which no record has yet been found.

How the evidence is marked

  • Filled blue — a record places the person there personally: a parish register, census, muster roll, hospital entry, marriage record.
  • Hollow orange — an inference, labelled as one: the unit or the household was demonstrably there, or the entry survives only as an index.
  • Grey dashed diamond — the claim itself is in doubt, not merely the location. A date that predates the register it supposedly comes from, for instance.
  • Grey dashed line — a route reconstructed from what was possible at the time, or a stretch of years with no record at all.

Both pages are self-contained: no scripts are loaded from anywhere else, no fonts or tiles are fetched, and nothing is tracked. Each map carries its own source table beneath it.

Compiled from records held by FamilySearch, the National Archives, the North Carolina State Archives, and the Baja California parish registers. Corrections are welcome.
